## Findings and decisions

- **Bell's Madras system (`monitorial-instruction`, existing 1796 node):** *Monitorial system*, “Madras system”; *Andrew Bell (educationalist)*, “Life and work.” Bell was superintendent of the Madras Male Orphan Asylum from 1789 to 1796 and observed pupil-teacher practices in local schools. The article identifies John Frisken, aged twelve years and eight months in 1796, as teaching with assistants and overseeing 91 boys. This supports 1796 as a documented Bell-system implementation, not as an origin date for local Pial schools or monitorial teaching generally. The existing title combines Bell and Lancaster despite the source describing independent, distinct methods. A correction is proposed to title the milestone “Bell's Madras monitorial system” and scope its summary to the dated asylum implementation.
- **Lancaster's Borough Road school (`lancasterian-instruction`, 1798):** *British and Foreign School Society*, “History.” The school opened in 1798 and used older pupils to teach younger pupils under Lancaster's method. The source distinguishes this arrangement from Bell's similar but distinct Madras system. The node marks a dated school implementation, not the invention of monitorial teaching.
- **Borough Road College male-apprentice training (`borough-road-college`, 1809):** *West London Institute of Higher Education*, “Establishment.” The college developed from Lancaster's Borough Road school and records male-apprentice training beginning in 1809. *British and Foreign School Society* says the teacher-training college was added in 1801. These passages do not establish that the college itself was founded in 1809, so the node represents the dated training attestation. Its link records the named school-based training route.
- **British and Foreign School Society (`british-foreign-school-society`, 1808):** *British and Foreign School Society*, lead and “History.” The predecessor Society for Promoting the Lancasterian System was formed in 1808 to continue Lancaster's lead and was renamed the British and Foreign School Society in 1814. The node's year marks the predecessor's founding; its summary gives the later name and the Society's role in non-denominational British schools.
- **National Society for Education of the Poor (`national-society-education-poor`, 1811):** *National Society for Promoting Religious Education*, “History”; *Andrew Bell (educationalist)*, “Life and work.” The society was formed in 1811 to set up schools using Bell's system. A proposed parent from the corrected 1796 Madras milestone records explicit adoption. The sources also say Bell described his experiment in a 1797 publication and that the method began appearing in English schools in 1798/99. The book is retained as evidence of the dissemination path, not added as a separate capability node.
- **French King's Council of State (`french-kings-council-state`, 1557):** *Conseil d'État*, “History”; *Conseil du roi*, “Royal Councils.” The Council of State article identifies the earlier King's Council as the 1799 Council's ancestor and describes legal advisers hearing claims against the Crown. The 1557 date belongs to the earlier royal Council of State form; it does not date Louis XIV's reorganization. The *Conseil du roi* article says the separate finance council arose around 1560 and was reunited with the State Council around 1600; Louis XIV reorganized royal council membership from 1661. The node summary is limited to the 1557 royal body and its judicial section. The proposed influence edge captures the documented succession and retained legal-advisory/adjudicative role despite the Revolution's institutional break.

## Rejected assumptions and open questions

- The proposed route from La Salle's 1681 teacher community to his 1685 Reims normal school remains unrepresented; the existing normal-school entry remains unconnected. The article documents both teacher training and the later school, but does not establish that the earlier teachers, organization, or teaching model transferred into the normal school. The proposed node and edge are deferred pending such evidence.
- The archive establishes that Bell observed pupil-teacher practice at Madras Pial schools but gives no founding date for those schools or the practice. No date is inferred from Bell's 1789–1796 observation period.
- The National and British and Foreign societies represent parallel organizational adoption of distinct methods. No path is proposed between them, and chronology alone does not establish cross-adoption.
- The *Madras College* article says Bell founded the named school in 1833 and that it took its name from his system, but it does not establish that a specific monitorial implementation at that school contributed to the existing broad `public-primary-education` node. No edge is proposed.
- The Conseil d'État predecessor path is a succession claim across the French Revolution, not uninterrupted organizational continuity. The integration owner should retain it only if the direct-contribution audit and source review accept that scoped institutional influence.
