## Supported findings and limits

- The Spear article reports wooden hunting spears at least 400,000 years old. Spear-throwers are a separate lever tool that increases a dart's launch distance and speed. The bow-and-arrow article describes likely arrowheads at Sibudu Cave around 72,000–60,000 years ago; the node is dated approximately 60,000 years and its summary preserves that uncertainty. This is archaeological evidence for likely arrow use, not a precise invention date.
- Composite bows and longbows are broad tools with hunting as well as military use, so they are classified as engineering. `mounted-archery` and `english-longbow-warfare` represent their specific combat applications in warfare. The Longbow article dates the Ötzi bow to about 3300 BCE and documents massed English and Welsh use at Crécy in 1346.
- Mail armor evidence includes surviving finds from the third century BCE. The Plate armour article describes European full plate developing in the late medieval period from transitional armor worn with mail. Steel is a direct plate material; mail's role is retained only for that documented transition, not as a universal prerequisite for every plate armor tradition.
- The Middle Ages gunpowder-artillery article records battlefield gunpowder use in 1132, but that report should not be labeled a secure first cannon. The European-cannon node is instead scoped to primitive European cannon used at the Battle of Crécy in 1346; it does not claim a global invention date. Hand cannon remains a separate 13th-century capability with gunpowder as its direct input. A source distinction between early fire-lances, hand cannons, and larger cannon remains useful for later refinement.
- Weapon development edges were retained only where the sources describe physical components, an explicit design transition, or a documented derivative. Generic firearm class membership and mere chronological precedence were removed. Smokeless powder, percussion-cap ignition, rifling, the machine gun, catapults, and counterweight trebuchets remain roots where this source pass did not establish a specific contributing parent.
- The Standing army article identifies the Assyrian army under Tiglath-Pileser III (745–727 BCE) as an early standing army; `standing-army` uses 745 BCE as its marker, with the ordinary uncertainty of a broad institutional origin. `conscription` marks French mass national service in the 1790s; military logistics uses the 1670 French office as a documented institutional milestone; the military academy node uses the Savoy Royal Academy's 1678 opening. The Prussian Staff College was re-founded in 1810, and its graduation was a prerequisite for appointment to the Prussian General Staff, formally established in 1814. Roman legion dates and early organization are broad and should be revisited if a narrower, better-sourced institutional milestone is desired.
- The military-aviation article documents the French observation balloon at Fleurus in 1794. The 1904 radio ship-detection experiment is classified under measurement: Hülsmeyer demonstrated detection of a ship in fog, but not its distance, and military officials rejected the device. Chain Home is the warfare milestone: Britain's network reached full-time operation in 1938 and was the first military radar system to become operational. HMS Argus became the first aircraft carrier with a full-length flight deck for wheeled aircraft in 1918.
- The V-2 article supports its 1942 successful-launch milestone and suborbital 1944 flight. It also documents postwar transfer of V-2 hardware, facilities, and expertise; Redstone is explicitly a direct V-2 descendant. The R-7 article describes its ICBM role and later launch-vehicle family. Specific adapters were added so the cross-domain route can be checked at the vehicle level: Sputnik-PS derives from R-7 and launched Sputnik 1; Vostok derives from R-7 for crewed flight. Redstone is a direct Mercury-Redstone contributor through its established engine and flight history, while Mercury-Redstone used the Jupiter-C first stage as its starting vehicle. Atlas is a distinct U.S. operational ICBM milestone dated 1959. No V-2-to-Shuttle shortcut is proposed.

The existing `crewed-spaceflight` node is dated 1961. Add `vostok-launch-vehicle -> crewed-spaceflight`, sourced to `https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vostok_(rocket_family)`, because the Vostok-K vehicle launched Vostok 1, the first crewed orbital spacecraft. Also consider `mercury-redstone-launch-vehicle -> crewed-spaceflight`, sourced to `https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercury-Redstone_Launch_Vehicle`, to represent the independent first American suborbital human flights in 1961; its direct contribution is the separate U.S. suborbital milestone, not the first human orbital flight.
