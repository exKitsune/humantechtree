## Construction

- **Scaffolding** — `Scaffolding`, history sections “Stone Age” and “Antiquity”: the article describes wall sockets near Lascaux paintings as evidence of elevated work platforms, and an early fifth-century-BCE Greek depiction; early systems used wood and rope. The proposal uses the Greek depiction as a conservative attestation date (500 BCE) and links woodworking to the timber poles and platforms. The Lascaux interpretation is earlier but inferential, so it does not set the node date.
- **Falsework** — `Falsework`, definition/history: distinguishes temporary structures that support permanent work from access scaffolds; describes Roman use in bridge and viaduct construction. The proposal uses 100 CE as approximate Roman-period attestation and keeps assembled timber members as the only parent; the arch link was removed because the evidence concerns how falsework supports arches, not a contribution from arch design to falsework. The article does not name the first use.
- **Concrete formwork** — `Formwork`, slab-formwork history: Roman concrete slabs and the Pantheon are described as having temporary forms or falsework made to the structure's intended shape. The Pantheon, Rome article says the building was probably dedicated around AD 126; the proposal uses that approximate attestation date and connects concrete's moldable state plus temporary structural support to shaping the casting. Formwork and falsework remain separate: one defines the mold; the other supports it.
- **Slipform construction** — `Slip forming`, overview/history: continuous or incrementally raised forms make uninterrupted concrete structures; vertical slipforming was in use by the early twentieth century for silos and grain elevators, while horizontal highway use began in the 1950s. The proposal uses 1900 as a decade-level approximate attestation and links concrete and formwork to the moving mold process.
- **Building information modeling** — `Building information modeling`, definition/history: digital representations combine geometry with functional information; initial building-modeling software appeared in the late 1970s and early 1980s. The proposal uses 1970 as an approximate decade marker for the concept, not the date of a functioning implementation, and links the concept to computer systems.

## Measurement and standards

- **Land surveying** — `Surveying`, definition/history: surveying determines terrestrial positions from distances and angles and is used for maps, property boundaries, and construction. The history section says the pyramids' north-south orientation, built around 2700 BCE, demonstrates Egyptian command of surveying. The proposal uses that approximate date as an attestation and keeps this early practice as a source node; later geometry and weights-and-measures milestones are not made its parents because they are dated later.
- **Triangulation surveying** — `Triangulation`, “History” and “In surveying”: Gemma Frisius proposed systematic surveying/cartographic triangulation in 1533; Snellius reworked the method in 1615 to estimate Earth's circumference. The proposal dates the operational geodetic milestone to Snellius's work in 1615 and uses geometric relationships as the mathematical input; the article's 1533 proposal and 1615 application are kept distinct from the general surveying practice node. The 1533 proposal is retained in this note to distinguish proposal from documented application.
- **Theodolite** — `Theodolite`, “Historical background” and “Development”: Digges's 1571 book uses the word for a horizontal-angle instrument, but the article calls Jonathan Sisson's 1725 instrument the first to combine the essential features of a modern theodolite, including a sighting telescope. The proposal uses 1725 for Jonathan Sisson's combined telescopic instrument with horizontal and vertical angle measurement. It remains standalone because the catalog has no evidenced, scope-matched predecessor among its existing nodes.
- **Gunter's chain surveying** — `Gunter's chain`, opening and “Method”: Gunter introduced the 66-foot chain divided into 100 links in 1620; it supported accurate plot measurements and made acreage calculations simple by defining ten square chains as an acre. The proposal uses 1620 and links the device to shared measures; the general surveying parent was removed because the article establishes use, not a direct historical contribution by the field itself. The chain was not a universal international standard; its unit system was statutory in England and later the British Empire.
- **Roman cadastral survey** — `Cadastre`, definition/history: cadastral surveys document property boundaries for valuation and taxation; a Roman Campania survey marker records an AD 77 survey used to recover state land and taxation. The proposal dates the specific Roman Campania survey to its AD 77 attestation; this is not presented as the origin of cadastres. The title distinguishes this Roman record from the general Land surveying node, and its parents are general surveying and taxation.
- **Geometric dimensioning and tolerancing** — `Geometric dimensioning and tolerancing`, “Origin”: the article credits Stanley Parker's 1940 work on design and inspection of mass-production engineering with the earliest published work on GD&T; the system specifies permitted variation in geometry and feature relationships. The proposal uses 1940 and links it to interchangeable production, whose fit requirements created a direct need for explicit tolerances.
- **Coordinate-measuring machine** — `Coordinate-measuring machine`, “Machine body” and opening: the first machine was developed by Ferranti in the 1950s; later CMM use measures part geometry against design intent and GD&T. The proposal uses 1950 as an approximate decade marker for Ferranti's first two-axis machine, which was developed to measure precision military components. It remains without a parent because the source does not establish that an existing catalog capability caused the machine. Three-axis versions followed in the 1960s, and computer control began in the early 1970s. GD&T was removed as a parent because it describes an inspection criterion, not the machine's historical cause.

## Logistics and supply

- **Palletized unit loads** — `Pallet`, opening/history: pallets group goods into a unit load for handling and storage; the article describes a 1924 lift-truck-platform patent and broader use with later forklift designs, accelerated by World War II logistics. The proposal uses 1924 for the patented platform, with the later wartime scale-up kept as context. The forklift is the supported direct contribution for lifting grouped loads; storage is an application of the pallet.
- **Barcode identification** — `Barcode`, “History”: the original barcode was patented in 1952; machine-readable railcar identification appeared in the late 1960s, and supermarket barcode adoption followed in the early 1970s, with the first UPC retail scan in 1974. The proposal uses the patent date for the invention and links the design to its documented Morse-code inspiration; it does not add computers as a parent. A separate 1974 UPC retail-scanning node captures the first retail scan, rather than changing the barcode invention date.
- **Heathrow LACES cargo-data interchange** — `Electronic data interchange`, definition/history: the Berlin Airlift's 1948 information burden helped develop early cargo-data exchange concepts, but the article describes London Heathrow's 1971 LACES cargo-processing scheme as one of the first integrated EDI systems. To avoid dating a later standardized machine-to-machine capability to the earlier concept, the proposal uses the 1971 LACES implementation and scopes it to cargo data and customs processing. Its title names this instance, and computer systems are its only parent; freight forwarding was a user of the system, not a demonstrated cause of its development.
- **Just-in-time inventory control** — `Lean manufacturing`, JIT description and postwar Japan: just-in-time focuses on receiving material when needed; the article describes postwar Japanese production conditions, Toyota's development of improvement teams after 1936, and inventory reduction. The proposal uses 1950 as an approximate postwar practice date, not as a precisely documented invention year. The scope is inventory timing and replenishment within production, rather than all manufacturing methods. It has logistics as its sole parent; the assembly-line relationship was removed because the source does not show that production-line methods caused JIT.
- **Supply-chain management** — `Supply chain management`, definition/origin: the article describes coordinated sourcing, production, logistics, information, and delivery, and says Keith Oliver introduced the term publicly in 1982. The proposal dates the named management practice to 1982. It includes logistics as an explicit direct input because SCM integrates storage and transport into wider sourcing and demand plans; EDI contributes the structured information flow. JIT was removed as a parent because the source presents it as one possible practice rather than evidence that it caused SCM's emergence.

## Rejected or deferred candidates

- Gauge blocks, containerization, caravanserai, surveying-adjacent broad metrology, warehouse administration, freight forwarding, granaries, and major bridges were already represented in the authoring graph, so none is duplicated here.
- Roman concrete was not added because the existing **Concrete** node already covers the ancient aggregate-and-binder material at approximately 300 BCE; splitting a named Roman subtype without a separate capability would duplicate its scope.
- Optical flats, sine bars, and dial indicators were reviewed but not added: the offline article extracts describe their functions, but did not provide a sufficiently specific historical date or an independent contribution path for this bounded proposal.
- Underpinning was deferred because the article defines a foundation-strengthening practice but the focused extract did not provide a defensible first-use date.
- Full article text stays in the ignored local cache. These notes preserve source sections, dates, limits, and rejected duplicates without claiming that unverified sections prove historical priority.
