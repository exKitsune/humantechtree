# Measurement expansion 3: machines that graduate instruments

Question: which documented manufacturing and measurement stages explain a path from precision screw cutting to survey results? Seven nodes in `data/measurement-expansion-3.json` and two supplemental edges in `data/connections.json` represent distinct components, machines, and measured results. The same source page can describe several separately dated milestones; no article-title uniqueness assumption merges their scopes.

## Evidence and dates

All article-body research used `F:/Wikipedia/wikipedia_en_all_maxi_2026-08.zim`, UUID `50e94998-c1ec-b5e7-ec34-499f1d907c30`. Relevant cached passages are in `.cache/measurement-expansion-3/` and the earlier `.cache/expansion-2-review/`.

- [Vernier scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vernier_scale), History and Functioning: the 1631 subsidiary scale interpolates between main-scale marks, on linear or angular instruments. It is a component distinct from a complete caliper. The existing caliper receives its readout contribution; [Theodolite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodolite), History, explicitly identifies vernier readout on Sisson's 1725 instrument. No caliper-to-theodolite ancestry is asserted.
- [Board of Longitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Board_of_Longitude), Origins: created by the 1714 Act to administer incentives for navigation methods. The article distinguishes its creation from the first meeting in 1737. [Dividing engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dividing_engine), History, specifies funding, publication, maker access, and teaching conditions attached to Ramsden's engine.
- [Screw-cutting lathe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Screw-cutting_lathe), Modern screw-cutting lathes: attributes Ramsden's leadscrew/slide-rest/change-gear machine to 1775 and explicitly says his increasingly accurate lathes produced a very accurate dividing engine. Leadscrew feed and lathe workholding perform separate roles; the retained lathe edge documents its independent rotation/cutting role despite the path through leadscrew. The existing 1800 screw-cutting lathe cannot be used as an input to this earlier machinery.
- [Jesse Ramsden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesse_Ramsden), Dividing engines: records the published description in 1777. The new engine's year is this documented publication milestone, not a claim of first construction in 1777. This avoids converting differing approximate construction dates in general histories into a precise invention date. Its immediate manufacturing input is Ramsden's screw-making capability, with the Board's funding/disclosure role independently represented.
- [Ramsden surveying instruments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramsden_surveying_instruments#Great_theodolite): the 1787 great theodolite used one of Ramsden's dividing engines to engrave its horizontal circle, a sighting telescope, and screw-adjusted microscope cross wires to interpolate readings. A machine that makes accurate instrument graduations is distinct from the instrument measuring angles. The later 1848 length micrometer is not the angular readout mechanism. No unverified Sisson-to-Ramsden design-transfer edge is added.
- [Anglo-French Survey (1784–1790)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-French_Survey_%281784%E2%80%931790%29), Hounslow base and survey account: a measured base in 1784, instrument observations in 1787–1788, and calculations in 1788–1790 have different dates. The base used calibrated glass rods and thermal correction. The 1790 result node receives the actual measured baseline, angle-measuring instrument, and triangulation method; glass is linked to the rod-based baseline measurement rather than skipping that intermediate to the final survey.

## Reusable lookup finding and limits

The earlier apparently empty Great theodolite entry was an HTML `meta refresh` to `Ramsden_surveying_instruments#Great_theodolite`, not missing source prose. The ignored `.cache/read-wiki-resolved.py` helper follows both libzim and archive-local HTML redirects, refuses external targets/loops, and reports the resolved article and fragment. The actual source was then read offline. This resolves the source-availability part of the prior deferral.

The later Board of Ordnance theodolite (1791) and national survey are deliberately left for a further batch: the first Royal Society instrument and second Ordnance instrument are distinct machines. Article support remains separate from independent historical verification; no `reviewed` field is set.
